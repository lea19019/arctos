# Q1 — Language emergence and target-language commitment

> Status: **7-model run complete** (2026-05-27 → 28). Aya 8B (Cohere),
> TowerBase 7B v0.1 (Llama-2 + CPT), TowerInstruct 7B v0.2 (+ MT-SFT),
> Tower-Plus 9B (Gemma 2 base + CPT + MT-SFT), BLOOM 7B1 (BigScience,
> ALiBi + LayerNorm), EuroLLM 9B (Llama + multilingual specialist),
> Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (Meta, mid-2024). n = 200 FLORES+ dev examples
> per pair × 3 pairs (cs→de, en→zh, en→arz). NLLB-3.3B deferred (needs
> encoder-decoder framework extension).

## Satisfied-when criterion (from PHASE1-PLAN)

> For each model and language pair: a chart and a sentence describing
> (a) the layer at which source-language identity becomes linearly
> decodable, (b) the layer at which target-language token mass dominates
> under logit lens, (c) the relationship between (a) and (b). Disagreements
> between probing and logit lens are reported, not hidden.

## Methods used

Four methods, all from-scratch in `src/interp/`, run on the same 600
calibration prompts per model:

- **Logit lens** (`logit_lens.py`) — `ln_final(resid_post[ℓ]) @ W_U` at
  the last prompt position, then `softmax → index_select` on the gold
  target-prefix token ids (first 8 ids of the FLORES target sentence,
  no special tokens). Output per example: per-layer probability mass on
  the gold prefix, shape `(N, L, K)`.
- **Probing** (`probing.py`) — Hewitt & Liang 2019 selectivity:
  per-layer linear probe accuracy *minus* control-task probe accuracy
  (random label assignment with same input distribution). Two probes
  per model:
  - `probing_target_id` — 3-class target language ID, probed at end of
    MT prompt. **Leaky** (the prompt names the target language), so
    accuracy is ≈1.0 at every layer. Selectivity is still meaningful as
    "where does the prompt-revealed info live most cleanly in the
    residual."
  - `probing_source_id` — 2-class source language ID (ces vs eng),
    probed at the end of *raw source text* (no MT instruction). Clean.
- **Information Flow Routes (IFR)** (`ifr.py`) — magnitude-based variant
  of Ferrando & Voita 2024: per-(layer, head) `|z_h @ W_O[h]|`, per-MLP
  `|mlp_out|`, and `|embed|` at the target position, L1-normalized per
  example, averaged across the calibration set. Reports per-layer,
  per-head, and per-MLP importance scores.
- **Direct logit attribution (DLA)** (`dla.py`) — decomposes the *target
  token's logit* at the final position into per-(layer, head),
  per-(layer, MLP), and embedding contributions via an RMSNorm-linearized
  projection through ln_final + W_U. Unlike IFR (magnitude, always
  non-negative), DLA is **signed**: positive contributions push toward
  the target token, negative contributions push away. Identifies
  interpretable "predictor" and "suppressor" heads.

Per-method validation runs on the same prompt set — disagreement is
expected and is the point of the cross-check.

## Cross-model results

### Logit lens — final-layer P(gold target prefix) (3-model)

| pair    | Aya   | TowerBase | TowerInstruct |
|---------|-------|-----------|---------------|
| cs→de   | 0.014 | 0.011     | 0.016         |
| en→zh   | 0.091 | 0.066     | 0.078         |
| en→arz  | 0.013 | **0.046** | **0.049**     |

The en→arz gap is **already present at the CPT stage**: TowerBase
(0.046) is already ~3.5× Aya (0.013) on this hard pair, and MT-SFT
adds only marginal further commitment (0.049). The bilingual CPT step,
which simply continued-pretrains on parallel and monolingual text in
the supported languages, is what gives Tower its handle on the hard
cross-script + register-shift pair. SFT helps a little; CPT does the
heavy lifting.

Aya's en→zh advantage (0.091 vs 0.078) is also worth noting — Aya was
trained on a multilingual mixture with much more Chinese exposure.
TowerInstruct closes some of that gap relative to TowerBase
(0.066 → 0.078), so MT-SFT helps on en→zh more than on cs→de or arz.

### Logit lens — mid-layer mass (mean over L14-18)

| pair    | Aya     | TowerBase | TowerInstruct |
|---------|---------|-----------|---------------|
| cs→de   | 0.0000  | 0.0004    | 0.0003        |
| en→zh   | 0.0028  | 0.0022    | 0.0030        |
| en→arz  | 0.0000  | 0.0022    | 0.0008        |

The early-mid commitment on cs→de and en→arz that Tower shows is
already in TowerBase — CPT, not SFT, is responsible. Curiously,
TowerBase has *more* mid-layer mass on en→arz than TowerInstruct
(0.0022 vs 0.0008): SFT pushes the commitment later in the stack on
that pair.

### IFR — per-layer importance shape (7-model)

Depth-partition shares of IFR layer mass:

| model              | first quarter | middle half | last quarter | notes |
|--------------------|---------------|-------------|--------------|---|
| Aya 8B             | 7-8%          | 35-39%      | 53-58%       | Cohere |
| TowerBase 7B       | 5-6%          | 45-52%      | 43-49%       | Llama-2 + CPT |
| TowerInstruct 7B   | 5-6%          | 44-52%      | 42-51%       | + MT-SFT |
| **Tower-Plus 9B**  | **~30%**      | ~52%        | **~18%**     | **outlier — Gemma 2 base** |
| BLOOM 7B1          | ~12%          | ~43%        | ~45%         | ALiBi + LayerNorm |
| EuroLLM 9B         | ~3%           | ~45%        | ~53%         | Llama + multilingual |
| Llama-3.1-8B       | ~5-8%         | ~45-50%     | ~45-50%      | Meta baseline |

**Headline finding for Q4 V2 (shared-depth hypothesis):**

*The depth signature generalizes across 6 of 7 architectures.* Aya
(Cohere), TowerBase, TowerInstruct (both Llama-2), BLOOM (BigScience,
ALiBi + LayerNorm), EuroLLM (Llama-3 + multilingual specialist), and
Llama-3.1 all show first-quarter mass ≤12%, middle-half ~40-50%, and
last-quarter dominant or co-dominant (~45-58%). This holds across:
- two normalization types (RMSNorm + LayerNorm),
- two positional encoding schemes (RoPE + ALiBi),
- two model generations (2022 BLOOM ↔ 2024 Llama-3.1),
- four distinct lineages (Cohere, Llama, BigScience, Llama-multilingual),
- three training intents (general LM, MT-SFT, multilingual specialist).

That's a meaningful generalization claim for a quantization paper:
*per-depth bit-budget allocation that protects the last quarter and
aggressively compresses the first quarter should generalize across the
Llama-class / BLOOM-class architecture families.*

**Tower-Plus 9B is the boundary of this generalization.** Its Gemma 2
base reshapes IFR magnitudes (extra pre-/post-feedforward layernorms +
logit softcapping suppress late-layer magnitudes). Within Tower-Plus,
the *rankings* are still meaningful; the *shape* differs. Two
implications:
1. The depth signature is **not** truly architecture-agnostic — Gemma 2
   breaks it.
2. A quantization method that uses the depth signature should test
   against Gemma 2-derived models specifically; the per-layer bit
   budget cannot just be "first quarter cheap, last quarter expensive"
   for every model.

**TowerBase ≈ TowerInstruct still holds.** Within the Llama-2 Tower
family, CPT (not SFT) determines the depth profile — confirmed across
IFR, lens, and DLA. SFT is a small refinement (≤1pp per quarter).

Top-5 most-important layers fall in the last ~20% of depth for every
non-Gemma-2 model (e.g., L26-31 in 32-layer models, L36-41 in 42-layer
EuroLLM).

### DLA — direct logit attribution

#### Layer-summed signed contribution to gold-target logit

| pair    | Aya         | TowerBase   | TowerInstruct |
|---------|-------------|-------------|---------------|
| cs→de   | 46.3        | 3.5         | 3.9           |
| en→zh   | 53.6        | 12.6        | 11.8          |
| en→arz  | 31.0        | 12.4        | 11.5          |

Aya's absolute DLA magnitudes are 4-10× Tower's. The cross-model gap is
likely an architectural / scaling artifact (different RMSNorm gamma
scales, different embedding norms), so absolute values aren't directly
comparable — but the *shape* across layers and the *ranking* of
components are.

#### Depth profile — Aya commits earlier than Tower

The cross-model DLA depth chart (`results/_combined/q1/dla_layer_combined.png`)
shows two distinct trajectories:

- **Aya**: positive DLA begins ramping up at ~70% depth, peaks at L31.
  Aya does target-pointing work distributed across the last ~10 layers.
- **TowerBase / TowerInstruct**: essentially flat until ~95% depth,
  then a sharp jump in the very final 1-2 layers. Tower defers
  almost all target-token commitment to the very end of the network.

Within each model, the 3 language pairs trace nearly identical
trajectories. Within the Tower family, TowerBase and TowerInstruct
overlap almost exactly — once again confirming CPT (not SFT)
determines the shape.

#### Per-head DLA: recurring "predictor" and "suppressor" heads (Aya)

Same heads appear in the top-N across pairs — these are pair-agnostic
MT-critical heads, exactly the kind of finding Q2 is designed to track:

| head      | role            | shows up in |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| L30.H8    | predictor (+)   | cs→de (top, +0.72), en→arz (#2, +0.44) |
| L29.H16   | suppressor (−)  | cs→de (top neg, −0.22), en→zh (top neg, −0.21) |
| L26.H5    | suppressor (−)  | en→zh (#4, −0.18), en→arz (top neg, −0.18) |
| L30.H15   | suppressor (−)  | cs→de (#3, −0.21), en→arz (#2, −0.06) |

L30.H8 is a strong cross-pair "target-language predictor" head; L29.H16
and L26.H5 are systematic suppressors that push away from the gold
target across multiple pairs. These are circuit-level findings that
activation patching (Q2) will be able to verify causally.

### Probing — selectivity

- **Target-language probe** (leaky): both models hit accuracy ≈1.0 at
  every layer. Selectivity peaks early: Aya at L4 (0.74), Tower at L0
  (0.73). The L0 / L4 split is the first hint of a real architectural
  difference, but the leak makes interpretation hard.
- **Source-language probe** (raw source, no instruction; 400 balanced
  examples, ces vs eng), 7 models:

  | model              | peak sel | peak layer | notes |
  |--------------------|----------|------------|---|
  | TowerBase 7B       | 0.600    | L0         | Llama-2 + bilingual CPT |
  | TowerInstruct 7B   | 0.562    | L0         | + MT-SFT |
  | Llama-3.1-8B       | 0.550    | L0         | Meta, mid-2024 |
  | BLOOM 7B1          | 0.550    | L0         | ALiBi, multilingual pretraining |
  | Aya 8B             | 0.550    | L17        | Cohere, multilingual pretraining |
  | EuroLLM 9B         | 0.537    | L9         | Llama + multilingual specialist |
  | Tower-Plus 9B      | 0.537    | L7         | Gemma 2 + bilingual CPT + MT-SFT |

  **All 7 models hover at 0.40-0.55 selectivity across all layers** —
  the differences between peaks are small (∼0.05-0.10). The *peak
  location* is real but the signal magnitude is broadly similar
  everywhere: source-language ID for a binary cs/en task is easily
  decodable from any layer's residual in any modern multilingual LLM.

  The 4-vs-3 split (L0 peak: TowerBase, TowerInstruct, Llama-3.1,
  BLOOM; mid-network peak: Aya at L17, EuroLLM at L9, Tower-Plus at
  L7) is real but not load-bearing for the quantization story. The
  practical takeaway: embedding-layer features carry meaningful
  language information across the board, so embedding quantization
  needs care everywhere; the specific layer where language ID is
  *most cleanly* decodable varies.

## Cross-method disagreement

Per PHASE1-PLAN, disagreement is reported, not hidden:

- Within Aya, the *layer* where source-language ID is most cleanly
  decodable (probing) and the *layer* where target-language mass starts
  to rise (logit lens) need not match. The first run had source-ID probe
  saturated and unusable; the re-run will give a real number.
- Within both models, IFR's top-ranked layer is the last layer (L31)
  for every pair, but the *shape* across all layers differs from the
  lens curve. IFR weights late layers naturally (magnitude variant); the
  finer story is in the distribution, not the argmax.

## Open caveats

1. **n = 200 per pair** is the small end for the lens-mass mean — the
   `target_mass.mean(0, 2)` curve has visible noise.
2. **Tower used Aya's generic prompt template** instead of Unbabel's
   SFT format. Tower's logit-lens / IFR numbers may be modestly
   handicapped because of this.
3. **IFR is magnitude-based**, not full attention-rollout attribution.
   Late layers are naturally heavier — the *relative* shape across
   layers is the load-bearing claim.
4. **omt-llama is a phantom**: PHASE1-PLAN's `facebook/omnilingual-mt-llama-8B`
   does not exist on HF. Substituted with TowerBase-7B-v0.1, which
   reframes the 3-model contrast as a within-family MT-specialization
   progression: general LM (Aya) → Llama-2 + bilingual CPT (TowerBase)
   → Llama-2 + CPT + MT-SFT (TowerInstruct).

## Bearing on Q4 (shared-depth hypothesis)

The 3-model series gives a sharper read on V2 (characteristic depth
signature that generalizes):

- The depth signature is *broadly similar* across two architectures
  (Aya: Cohere, Tower: Llama-2) and three language pairs — first-
  quarter mass is ~6-8% everywhere, last-quarter dominates, and the
  top-2 most-important layers (L30, L31) match in all three models.
- The signature is *not identical*: the Aya→Tower transition pushes
  ~10 percentage points of mass from the last quarter into the middle
  half — but the TowerBase→TowerInstruct ablation locates this change
  in the **CPT step**, not the MT-SFT step. SFT changes the depth
  profile by ≤1 percentage point per quarter.
- Source-language ID *placement* matches the same story: Tower-family
  models peak at L0 (embedding), Aya peaks at L17 (mid-network) — and
  again, both TowerBase and TowerInstruct peak at L0, so this is
  CPT-induced.

For Q4 V2/V3: continued pretraining on bilingual data appears to be the
single intervention that most reshapes the depth signature. SFT on top
acts as a small refinement that preserves the CPT-shaped profile. If
this generalizes (which Q4 will test on more architectures), then
"depth signature is stable within a training-recipe family" is a much
sharper claim than "depth signature is stable across architectures."
