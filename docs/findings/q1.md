# Q1 — Language emergence and target-language commitment

> Status: in progress. Aya 8B + TowerInstruct 7B v0.2 results landed
> 2026-05-27. TowerBase-7B-v0.1 (within-family MT-SFT ablation, substituted
> for the phantom omt-llama) is running. n = 200 FLORES+ dev examples per
> language pair × 3 pairs (cs→de, en→zh, en→arz).

## Satisfied-when criterion (from PHASE1-PLAN)

> For each model and language pair: a chart and a sentence describing
> (a) the layer at which source-language identity becomes linearly
> decodable, (b) the layer at which target-language token mass dominates
> under logit lens, (c) the relationship between (a) and (b). Disagreements
> between probing and logit lens are reported, not hidden.

## Methods used

Three methods, all from-scratch in `src/interp/`, run on the same 600
calibration prompts per model:

- **Logit lens** (`logit_lens.py`) — `ln_final(resid_post[ℓ]) @ W_U` at
  the last prompt position, then `softmax → index_select` on the gold
  target-prefix token ids (first 8 ids of the FLORES target sentence,
  no special tokens). Output per example: per-layer probability mass on
  the gold prefix, shape `(N, L, K)`.
- **Probing** (`probing.py`) — Hewitt & Liang 2019 selectivity:
  per-layer linear probe accuracy *minus* control-task probe accuracy
  (random label assignment with same input distribution). Two probes
  per model:
  - `probing_target_id` — 3-class target language ID, probed at end of
    MT prompt. **Leaky** (the prompt names the target language), so
    accuracy is ≈1.0 at every layer. Selectivity is still meaningful as
    "where does the prompt-revealed info live most cleanly in the
    residual."
  - `probing_source_id` — 2-class source language ID (ces vs eng),
    probed at the end of *raw source text* (no MT instruction). Clean.
- **Information Flow Routes (IFR)** (`ifr.py`) — magnitude-based variant
  of Ferrando & Voita 2024: per-(layer, head) `|z_h @ W_O[h]|`, per-MLP
  `|mlp_out|`, and `|embed|` at the target position, L1-normalized per
  example, averaged across the calibration set. Reports per-layer,
  per-head, and per-MLP importance scores.

Per-method validation runs on the same prompt set — disagreement is
expected and is the point of the cross-check.

## Cross-model results

### Logit lens — final-layer P(gold target prefix)

| pair    | Aya (general LM) | Tower (CPT + MT-SFT) | Tower / Aya |
|---------|------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| cs→de   | 0.014            | 0.016                | 1.1× — tie  |
| en→zh   | 0.091            | 0.078                | 0.9× — Aya slightly higher |
| en→arz  | 0.013            | **0.049**            | **3.7× — Tower wins big** |

The single largest cross-model contrast is on en→arz: Tower puts ~4×
more mass on the gold Egyptian Arabic prefix than Aya does. MT-SFT
appears to help disproportionately on the cross-script + register-shift
pair. The standard pairs (cs→de, en→zh) are roughly tied.

### Logit lens — mid-layer mass (mean over L14-18)

| pair    | Aya     | Tower    |
|---------|---------|----------|
| cs→de   | 0.0000  | 0.0003   |
| en→zh   | 0.0028  | 0.0030   |
| en→arz  | 0.0000  | 0.0008   |

Tower has measurable mid-layer mass on cs→de and en→arz where Aya is
exactly zero. Tower commits to target-language tokens earlier.

### IFR — per-layer importance shape

Both models concentrate IFR mass in the last few layers, but Tower is
more diffuse:

| model | first quarter (L0-7) | middle half (L8-23) | last quarter (L24-31) |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Aya   | 7-8%                | 35-39%              | 53-58%                 |
| Tower | 5-6%                | 44-52%              | 42-51%                 |

Same *broad* shape (~6% first / ~50% middle-or-late / dominant last) but
the Aya→Tower transition pushes ~10 percentage points of mass out of the
last quarter into the middle half. Both models' top-5 most-important
layers fall within layers 26-31 across every pair.

### Probing — selectivity

- **Target-language probe** (leaky): both models hit accuracy ≈1.0 at
  every layer. Selectivity peaks early: Aya at L4 (0.74), Tower at L0
  (0.73). The L0 / L4 split is the first hint of a real architectural
  difference, but the leak makes interpretation hard.
- **Source-language probe** (raw source, no instruction; 400 balanced
  examples, ces vs eng):

  | model              | peak sel | peak layer | sel at L0 | sel at L17 | sel at L31 |
  |--------------------|----------|------------|-----------|------------|------------|
  | Aya 8B             | 0.550    | **L17**    | 0.475     | 0.462      | 0.450      |
  | TowerInstruct 7B   | 0.562    | **L0**     | 0.562     | 0.450      | 0.462      |

  This is a real architectural fingerprint. Tower's bilingual CPT pulls
  per-language token statistics into the embedding layer — the
  embedding alone is the most cleanly decodable layer for source-
  language ID. Aya builds source-language identity up gradually across
  the first ~half of the network, peaking around L17.

  Both models hover near 0.45-0.55 across all layers — well above the
  chance baseline (0.5 raw accuracy on a balanced binary task → 0.0
  selectivity if the control task matched), but not at ceiling. The
  source-language signal is *present* in the residual stream at every
  layer, just most cleanly extractable at the per-model peak.

## Cross-method disagreement

Per PHASE1-PLAN, disagreement is reported, not hidden:

- Within Aya, the *layer* where source-language ID is most cleanly
  decodable (probing) and the *layer* where target-language mass starts
  to rise (logit lens) need not match. The first run had source-ID probe
  saturated and unusable; the re-run will give a real number.
- Within both models, IFR's top-ranked layer is the last layer (L31)
  for every pair, but the *shape* across all layers differs from the
  lens curve. IFR weights late layers naturally (magnitude variant); the
  finer story is in the distribution, not the argmax.

## Open caveats

1. **n = 200 per pair** is the small end for the lens-mass mean — the
   `target_mass.mean(0, 2)` curve has visible noise.
2. **Tower used Aya's generic prompt template** instead of Unbabel's
   SFT format. Tower's logit-lens / IFR numbers may be modestly
   handicapped because of this.
3. **IFR is magnitude-based**, not full attention-rollout attribution.
   Late layers are naturally heavier — the *relative* shape across
   layers is the load-bearing claim.
4. **omt-llama is a phantom**: PHASE1-PLAN's `facebook/omnilingual-mt-llama-8B`
   does not exist on HF. Substituted with TowerBase-7B-v0.1, which
   reframes the 3-model contrast as a within-family MT-specialization
   progression: general LM (Aya) → Llama-2 + bilingual CPT (TowerBase)
   → Llama-2 + CPT + MT-SFT (TowerInstruct).

## Early bearing on Q4 (shared-depth hypothesis)

This is preliminary, single-question evidence, but it points in the V2
direction:

- The depth signature is *similar* across two architectures (Aya:
  Cohere, Tower: Llama-2) and across all three language pairs — first-
  quarter mass is ~6-8% everywhere, last-quarter dominates, and the
  exact top-2 layers (L30, L31) match in both models.
- The signature is *not identical*: MT-SFT shifts ~10 percentage points
  of mass from the last quarter into the middle half. That is
  detectable across all 3 pairs.

If TowerBase falls between Aya and TowerInstruct on the depth-partition
spectrum, the within-family ablation will support a "MT-SFT diffuses
work into mid-layers" claim. If TowerBase looks like TowerInstruct, the
diffusion is from CPT, not SFT. The 3-step series is designed to
distinguish these.

  (key `target_mass`, shape `(200, 32, 8)`).
