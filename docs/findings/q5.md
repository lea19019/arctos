# Q5 — Importance vs quantization sensitivity (the bridge to phase two)

> Status: **all 6 models complete** (2026-05-28). aya-expanse-8b,
> bloom-7b1, eurollm-9b-instruct, llama-3.1-8b-instruct, tower-plus-9b,
> gemma-3-12b-it. n = 50 calibration examples per pair, 3 pairs, 12 heads
> per group (top-|DLA| / bottom-|DLA| / random).

## The question

Does component importance (as flagged by DLA) predict quantization
sensitivity? If yes, an interpretability-guided bit budget is principled:
protect high-importance components, aggressively quantize the rest. If no,
importance and sensitivity are different axes — and the prior BYU paper
explicitly warned they might be.

## VERDICT (confirmed across two metrics): importance ≠ sensitivity

Tested at two levels, both null:

| experiment | what's perturbed | metric | mean ρ(importance, sensitivity) |
|------------|------------------|--------|----------------------------------|
| per-head (this doc) | one head's o_proj cols | gold-target logit drop | **−0.025** (n=18, t=−0.55) |
| per-layer (q5-strong) | whole layer's weights | **chrF++ drop on real translations** | **−0.065** (IFR), −0.046 (DLA), n=17 |

The stronger experiment — whole-layer perturbation measured by actual
translation quality (chrF++), not logit — was built specifically to rule
out "the metric was too weak." It gives the **same near-zero correlation**.
That settles it: **interpretability importance (IFR/DLA) does not predict
quantization sensitivity.** Reading 1 below is confirmed; Reading 2 is
ruled out.

Per-layer chrF++ depth-block drops vary wildly by model with no universal
pattern (eurollm: first-quarter fragile; gemma: uniformly fragile; aya:
perturbation *helps*; tower-plus en-arz: last-quarter fragile). There is no
model-agnostic "protect these layers" rule derivable from importance.

**Consequence for phase two:** the quantization method must allocate bits
using a **sensitivity-native signal** — direct per-component noise probing,
inverse-Hessian (GPTQ/SparseGPT), or activation magnitude (AWQ) — *not*
interpretability importance. Interpretability (Q1–Q4) earns its place by
*explaining how MT works and showing the depth signature generalizes*, not
by driving bit allocation. This is a clean, honest split and exactly the
dissociation the prior paper warned about.

The per-head logit-level analysis below is retained for the record; the
layer-level chrF++ result (`results/{model}/q5/q5_quality_summary.json`,
runner `quality_sensitivity.py`) is the authoritative one.

## Method

Per model, per language pair:
1. Rank attention heads by |DLA| (signed direct logit attribution to the
   gold target token) from the model's own Q1 results.
2. Form three head groups: top-12 by |DLA| (predicted critical), bottom-12
   (predicted unimportant), 12 random (control).
3. **Noise-sensitivity sweep**: add Gaussian noise to each head's output
   projection (`o_proj`) columns at σ ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5},
   measure the drop in gold-target-token logit.
4. Headline metric: **Spearman ρ between per-head |DLA| and per-head
   logit-drop at σ=0.5.**
5. Cross-checks: attribution patching (causal) and AWQ activation stats on
   the same calibration data.

## Result: importance does NOT predict per-head noise sensitivity

| model | cs→de | en→zh | en→arz |
|-------|-------|-------|--------|
| aya-expanse-8b | +0.001 | −0.182 | +0.393 |
| bloom-7b1 | −0.355 | −0.292 | +0.134 |
| eurollm-9b-instruct | −0.067 | −0.078 | +0.035 |
| llama-3.1-8b-instruct | +0.204 | −0.023 | +0.067 |
| tower-plus-9b | −0.024 | −0.309 | −0.109 |
| gemma-3-12b-it | −0.013 | −0.105 | +0.268 |

**Aggregate (18 cells, 6 models): mean ρ = −0.025, median −0.024,
std 0.19, 7/18 positive, one-sample t = −0.55 vs 0.** The correlation is
statistically **indistinguishable from zero**, with model- and
pair-dependent scatter. The `top/random` sensitivity ratios swing from
−3.9 to +9.1 — meaningless, because every group's mean logit-drop is at
the measurement-noise floor (~0.002–0.03 logit units).

**Conclusion at this measurement level:** the attention heads DLA flags as
most important for producing the target token are *not* reliably the heads
most sensitive to quantization noise.

## Two readings — and why we can't yet distinguish them

### Reading 1 (genuine): importance ≠ sensitivity
This is exactly the dissociation the prior paper warned about. If it holds
under a stronger metric, the thesis pivots cleanly: use interpretability
(Q1–Q4, which gave robust results) to *understand* MT, but use a
sensitivity-native signal (inverse-Hessian / AWQ activation magnitude) to
*allocate bits*. That is an honest, publishable result.

### Reading 2 (artifact): the per-head logit metric is too weak
Three reasons to suspect the metric, not the hypothesis:
1. **Magnitudes at noise floor.** Logit drops are ~0.002–0.03 — barely
   above measurement noise, so Spearman is dominated by noise.
2. **Tiny weight fraction.** Perturbing one head's `o_proj` columns
   touches a sliver of the model; real quantization degrades whole
   matrices.
3. **Wrong target metric.** We measure mean target-token *logit*, not
   *translation quality* (chrF++/COMET) — the quantity the method cares
   about. A logit can barely move while quality shifts, or vice versa.

## The deciding follow-up

The fix is cheap and resolves the ambiguity:
- Perturb **whole layers** (or whole attention/MLP sub-blocks), not
  single-head `o_proj` columns — match what quantization actually degrades.
- Measure **chrF++ drop on generated translations**, not logit drop.
- Keep the group design, but rank by IFR *and* DLA *and* AWQ to see which
  (if any) predicts the quality drop.

If that stronger experiment still shows null correlation, Reading 1 is
solid and the phase-two method uses a sensitivity-native signal. If it
shows correlation, the per-head logit metric was simply too blunt and
interpretability-guided allocation is back on the table.

## Cross-checks (recorded, not yet analyzed in depth)

- **Attribution patching** ran on lexical-substitution clean/corrupt pairs;
  the equal-token-length filter kept 10–36 of 50 pairs per cell (en-zh and
  en-arz cleaner than cs-de). Per-(layer, head) causal maps saved to
  `results/{model}/q5/attribution_{pair}.npz` for later comparison against
  the DLA and noise rankings.
- **AWQ activation stats** (per-channel q99 |activation|) saved to
  `results/{model}/q5/awq_stats_{pair}.npz`. Phase-two overlap analysis:
  do AWQ-salient channels coincide with high-DLA heads, or are they
  orthogonal? Orthogonality would mean the two signals are complementary.

## Bearing on the thesis

Q1–Q4 established that the MT depth signature generalizes across
architectures (strong positive result). Q5 says that signature is about
*where computation happens*, not necessarily *where precision is fragile* —
at least not at the per-head logit-drop level. The phase-two method's
allocation signal is therefore still open, and the layer-level + chrF++
experiment is the next thing that should run.
