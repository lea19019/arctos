# Adversarial review — Arm A / H1 (quantization), proposal v3, 2026-09-05

## (a) Verdict: SURVIVES WITH CHANGES

The question ("does sink handling move the per-language gap?") is real and unasked in print, and the lab's deployment turns out to be exactly the regime H1 speaks to: CTranslate2 int8 quantizes activations per row at runtime (`src/layers/common.cc`: `_quantize_op(input, qinput, qinput_scale)` precedes `_gemm_op`), so "NLLB in int8" *is* dynamic W8A8, not weight-only. But as designed the arm cannot decide (a) vs (b), two of its six cells are no-ops, the expected effect at 8-bit is below the per-language COMET interval, and the gap the lab has actually replicated (4-bit/2-bit weight-only, en→X) is one H1 has nothing to say about — the registry's `rtn+SW ≈ rtn` already killed the weight-side story. Redesigned around quantizer *granularity* rather than "sink exempt or not", it is decidable in budget.

## (b) Objections

**1. No measured gap to close at W8A8.** Marie & Fujita test 4- and 2-bit only. Marchisio (Table 1/6) has per-token W8A8 at 35B/103B: −0.8% average, W8A8 *helps* at 35B, W8A8-SQ −0.5%. The repo's own 8B en→X 4-bit weight-only spread is ~0.5 (fr) to ~3–6 (ml) COMET; at 8-bit per-token it will be a fraction of that, inside the ±0.6–1.0 per-language bootstrap CI at n≈960. A gap-of-gaps between two conditions is wider still. *Fix:* pilot first — W8A8-naive on TowerBase, 4 languages, paired bootstrap over sentences (same sources across conditions; independent bootstraps waste half the power). Pre-register a minimum detectable gap. If the naive gap is inside the CI, move the arm to the regime where sinks are known to bite in English: per-tensor static A8 or A4 (PrefixQuant's own headline is per-tensor static).

**2. The 2×3 grid has two empty cells and the sub-mechanisms collapse.** Under per-token dynamic quantization there are no calibration-fitted activation scales, so "English vs language-matched calibration" is undefined for the naive and sink-exempt conditions; only SmoothQuant has a calibration set. And (b) — "per-channel scales dominated by massive channels" — does not exist per-token: the massive channel sets the scale of *its own row only*, so "sink exempt" is a one-row change and is the same intervention as (a). The discriminating prediction is therefore untestable in the deployed regime. *Fix:* factor the design as granularity {per-token dynamic = CT2/bnb regime; per-tensor static = where (b) lives} × sink treatment × calibration language (static only). State that (a) is tested in the dynamic regime and (b) only in the static one.

**3. "Exempt the sink token" changes more than the constant.** Leaving position 0 in FP16 at the q/k/v inputs changes the sink's K and V for every query, i.e. the attention pattern, and the KV-cache precision of that row (PrefixQuant keeps the prefix KV in the cache — decide whether you do). The right isolation of (a) is Yu §4.1: replace the massive channels at the sink with the tensor median, quantize, restore in FP16 — channel-level, not token-level. Two controls are missing: exempt a position-matched non-sink token (one-FP16-row null), and exempt the sink's *non*-massive channels only. Note bitsandbytes LLM.int8 (threshold 6.0) already routes outlier channels to FP16 — the lab's `bnb_int8` NLLB variant is a free "massive channels exempt" arm.

**4. Partly answered; overclaimed as "no paper has tested an activation-level cause".** Yu §4.1 Table 3: median-replace-restore retains 71–83% of SmoothQuant's W8A8 gain on Llama-7/13/30B (English) — so (a)-style ≈ SmoothQuant is known; only the per-language increment is new. arXiv:2601.18306 (Calibrating Beyond English, Llama-3.1-8B/Qwen2.5-7B, 10 languages) attributes per-language GPTQ/AWQ failures to cross-language *activation-range mismatch* at layers 0 and last — that is (b), already stated and partially measured (weight-only). Marchisio splits W8A8 vs W8A8-SQ by Ltn/IE vs not. Nothing per-language on sink handling exists; say exactly that.

**5. Deployment mismatch stated backwards.** GGUF/AWQ/AutoRound — the lab's replicated gap — are weight-only; activations stay FP16; H1 is silent there, and the weight-side version (`rtn+SW ≈ rtn`, 8 models) is dead. The "weight-only 4-bit reference" cell measures a different phenomenon and must not be pooled. *Fix:* write H1 as a claim about *activation* quantization (CT2/bnb int8, the NLLB deployment), and drop the implication that it explains Marie & Fujita.

**6. Feasibility.** No library does W8A8/SmoothQuant/PrefixQuant for M2M100/NLLB; llm-compressor and PrefixQuant's repo are Llama-family. Fake-quant on `nn.Linear` (per-token absmax input QDQ, per-channel weight QDQ, optional SmoothQuant scale migration) is architecture-agnostic and one week for both; say "simulated, not kernels". NLLB needs the sink *defined* per stack: decoder position 0 is `</s>`, position 1 the language tag (Mutisya & Mugane 2026 find 83–91% cross-attention mass on `</s>`/tags), encoder unknown — Arm C must run first. Compute: 3 models × ~6 conditions × 40 directions × 960 sentences ≈ 700k translations plus COMET; cut to en→X, 10 directions.

**7. Statistics and confounds.** n=20 languages with script, family and fertility collinear gives a slope CI of roughly ±0.4 in ρ — the covariate test is exploratory, say so. Tower officially covers ~10 languages; unsupported FLORES directions floor and inflate "low-resource". Quantization is deterministic; the seed is the calibration draw — take ≥3. Language-matched calibration changes token count and sequence length; match tokens, not sentences. COMET-22 is least reliable exactly where the gap is (Marchisio: 10× human/automatic discrepancy); pair with chrF++ and language-ID. Registry: en→X only; X→en did not replicate.

## (c) Cut / keep / sharpen

Cut: the calibration-language axis for dynamic conditions; the weight-only cell as a "reference"; X→en. Keep: TowerBase + NLLB-3.3B, paired-bootstrap per-language deltas, language-ID. Sharpen: granularity as the factor; Yu-style channel restore as the (a) probe; bnb int8 as a free existing arm; a pre-registered minimum detectable gap.

## (d)

**Most decidable:** does per-token W8A8 (the CT2 regime) produce any per-language gap on TowerBase en→X, and does FP16-restoring the massive channels at the sink remove it? **Least decidable:** the (a)-vs-(b) discrimination via calibration language — (b) has no calibration in the deployed regime, and both interventions alter the sink's K/V.
